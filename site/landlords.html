How the score works

  • Politics (0–100): 40% the governor taking office in 2027, 40% control of the legislature after the Nov 3, 2026 election (split or coalition counts half), 20% the 2024 presidential margin, as a check on which way the state leans over time.
  • Property tax (0–100): the average of two rates: what homeowners pay (Tax Foundation, 2024 taxes) and what a $600,000 apartment building pays in the state's largest city (Lincoln Institute, 2025). Rentals don't get homestead breaks, so the second rate is closer to a landlord's bill. Lowest in the country scores 100, highest scores 0.
  • Tenant law (0–100): 35% rent regulation (statewide cap 0, local rent control in force 30, allowed but unused 70, banned by the state 100), 45% the eviction process (cost, speed and notice periods, from SoldFast's 2026 index), 20% whether the state requires just cause to end a tenancy.
  • The overall score is the weighted average of the three, using the sliders above. Equal weights by default.

Before you buy

  • 2026 results are unofficial. Kansas's governor's race was still uncalled on Nov 6 and counts half; Alaska's legislature runs on bipartisan coalitions and counts as split.
  • Politics is a lean, not a law. A state can swing in one election, as Texas, Ohio and Georgia did this week, but statutes like rent-control bans usually outlast a governor.
  • The tax rate for the largest city can be far from yours: Detroit and Jackson pull Michigan and Mississippi down. Check the county where you'd buy.
  • Cities can be much stricter than their state (St. Paul, Seattle, Los Angeles). This is a starting point, not legal or financial advice.
